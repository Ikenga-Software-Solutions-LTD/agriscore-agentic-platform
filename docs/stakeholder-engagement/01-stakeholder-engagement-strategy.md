# AgriScore Guyana Pilot — Stakeholder Engagement Strategy

**Prepared for:** Libertas Alpha Technologies  
**Project:** AgriScore Guyana Maize and Ginger Pilot  
**Status:** Meeting-preparation draft  
**Purpose:** Turn stakeholder meetings into clear pilot commitments, not general expressions of interest.

## 1. The objective of stakeholder engagement

The purpose of the first stakeholder meetings is to establish a **small, credible technical and operating coalition** for an officer-led maize and ginger feasibility pilot in Guyana. The immediate objective is not to sell software, seek an endorsement before evidence exists, or ask partners to share sensitive data. It is to agree the minimum responsibilities, governance, and field evidence needed to test the platform properly.

> **The central ask:** Help us design and validate a controlled pre-plant feasibility pilot for maize and ginger that supports farmers and officers with evidence, while keeping professional judgment and public agricultural safeguards in place.

The desired result is a named technical focal point, a short working session, a local crop-profile review path, an evidence-collection protocol, and a practical route to a small supervised pilot cohort.

## 2. Pilot message for every stakeholder

AgriScore is an evidence and decision-support platform for pre-plant crop feasibility. It organises plot, soil, climate, crop, and market evidence; shows what is missing; calculates an explainable feasibility result; and prepares a review record for qualified agricultural professionals.

It is **not** an automated lending platform, insurance system, buyer-matching service, procurement channel, contract engine, or replacement for extension services. The first Guyana pilot is restricted to maize and ginger, and both crop profiles remain subject to local agronomic approval before they can become decision-support ready.

## 3. Stakeholder map and primary asks

| Stakeholder group | Why they matter | Primary ask | Desired first commitment | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| **NAREI — research, crop development and extension** | NAREI is positioned to support agricultural research, extension, crop diversification, field crops and spices, making it the core technical partner candidate.[1] | Co-design and review the maize/ginger crop profiles, evidence requirements, and field workflow. | Name a technical focal point; agree a 60–90 minute technical scoping workshop; identify appropriate review functions. | P0 |
| **NAREI soil-testing function** | The NAREI soil chemical services capability is relevant to crop selection, fertiliser application, and soil-management evidence.[2] | Define an acceptable soil-sampling/report pathway and minimum report metadata for the pilot. | Confirm the preferred testing/collection route, report fields, units, and expected turnaround. | P0 |
| **Hydrometeorological Service** | Hydromet is Guyana’s official provider of weather, water, and climate information.[3] | Identify appropriate weather/climate products, geographic resolution, use conditions, and a focal point for the pilot. | Agree a data-discovery session and establish whether manual, public, or formal data-access arrangements are appropriate. | P0 |
| **Guyana Marketing Corporation (GMC) / market partners** | Market evidence needs credible, dated, crop-form-specific observations; GMC is a relevant market-development partner candidate.[4] | Define maize and ginger commodity forms, acceptable market sources, collection frequency, and possible buyer/market observation protocol. | Name a market focal point and agree a simple observation template for a pilot. | P0 |
| **Ministry of Agriculture leadership / Permanent Secretary** | Ministry engagement can ensure alignment, introductions, and appropriate governance without prematurely converting the pilot into a national programme.[5] | Seek strategic awareness, appropriate institutional introductions, and clarity on the responsible operating route. | Confirm whether the proposed engagement pathway is appropriate and identify any required approvals. | P1 |
| **Guyana School of Agriculture (GSA)** | GSA supports agricultural technical capacity and links education, research, extension, farmers, and agricultural professionals.[6] | Explore future training, simulation, and evidence-collection capacity. | Identify a contact for a later training/workflow-design conversation. | P1 |
| **National Plant Protection Organization (NPPO)** | Ginger safeguards may require plant-health context; NPPO functions include pest/disease surveillance and agricultural commodity inspection.[7] | Seek advice or referral on plant-health/planting-material safeguards when the ginger profile is drafted. | Identify the appropriate technical route; no broad data request in the first contact. | P1 |
| **Pilot farmer/community and extension partners** | They determine whether the workflow is practical and whether evidence can be collected respectfully. | Co-design consent, onboarding, plot evidence collection, and follow-up. | Identify a small potential cohort only after the technical protocol is ready. | P0, later in sequence |

## 4. Recommended engagement sequence

| Step | Meeting / engagement | Goal | Exit condition |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **NAREI introductory meeting** | Establish technical fit, present safeguards, and secure a focal point. | Agreement to a focused technical scoping session or a clear referral to the right team. |
| 2 | **NAREI technical workshop** | Define maize/ginger crop-profile ownership, soil/plant-health evidence, and review workflow. | Draft technical workplan, named reviewers, and unresolved-questions log. |
| 3 | **Hydromet data-discovery discussion** | Identify appropriate climate/water information and use conditions. | Documented data pathway, known constraints, and technical contact. |
| 4 | **GMC / market-data discussion** | Define market evidence protocol by crop form and location. | Pilot market-observation template and source/collection owner. |
| 5 | **Ministry alignment briefing** | Confirm appropriate operating/governance route and required institutional steps. | Guidance on introductions, approvals, and pilot alignment. |
| 6 | **GSA / training discussion** | Explore training and simulated workflow support. | Optional training/capacity-building workstream. |
| 7 | **Pilot operating workshop** | Consolidate partners, locations, roles, consent, and pilot schedule. | Signed or documented pilot-readiness decision and first cohort plan. |

## 5. What we are asking for—and what we are not asking for

| We are asking for | We are not asking for |
|---|---|
| Technical guidance and local validation | An immediate institutional endorsement or national roll-out |
| A named focal point and working session | Sensitive farmer data or unrestricted system/data access |
| Advice on evidence standards and workflows | A request to replace existing extension or research services |
| Appropriate data-use and consent guidance | An automated credit, insurance, buyer, or procurement decision |
| A small, supervised pilot route | A commitment before the partner understands the pilot |

## 6. Desired commitments by the end of the first engagement round

The first engagement round is successful if the project secures the following outcomes:

| Outcome | Minimum standard |
|---|---|
| Technical focal point | One named contact at NAREI or a formal referral to the relevant crop/soil/extension function. |
| Crop-profile review route | Agreed owner and review process for maize and ginger profiles. |
| Soil-evidence route | Defined sampling/report protocol and a pathway to verified soil evidence. |
| Climate-evidence route | Appropriate Hydromet engagement or a documented approved interim approach. |
| Market-evidence route | At least one agreed source/collection method for dated maize and ginger market observations. |
| Pilot operating route | Initial direction on locations, extension support, consent, and potential cohort selection. |
| Governance record | Meeting notes, actions, owners, target dates, and explicit unresolved risks. |

## 7. Questions every meeting must answer

The meeting facilitator should aim to leave every conversation with answers to these questions:

1. Who is the right technical or operational owner for this work?
2. What can this stakeholder credibly contribute during a small pilot?
3. What evidence, standards, or approvals are required before a real assessment is used?
4. What should not be assumed or automated?
5. What is the next action, who owns it, and by what date?
