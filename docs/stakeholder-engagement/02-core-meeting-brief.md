# AgriScore Guyana Pilot — Core Meeting Brief

**Prepared by:** Chinedu Jamike, Libertas Alpha Technologies  
**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Meeting purpose:** Explore a technical collaboration for a controlled maize and ginger pre-plant feasibility pilot in Guyana.

## The opportunity

Farmers and agricultural officers often need to bring together separate pieces of information before planting: plot conditions, soil results, climate/water context, crop requirements, and evidence of the intended market. When this information is incomplete or difficult to interpret, valuable decisions can be delayed or made with avoidable uncertainty.

**AgriScore** is being built to organise this evidence into a clear, traceable feasibility assessment. It helps users understand what supports a crop plan, what is uncertain, what data is missing, and what should be reviewed by a qualified agricultural professional before planting.

## The proposed Guyana pilot

| Item | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Country | Guyana |
| Initial crops | Maize and ginger |
| Decision point | Before planting, for a specific crop–plot–planting-window combination |
| Main users | Farmers supported by trained field/extension officers, with agronomic review |
| Initial scale | Up to 40 supervised assessments, balanced across crops where feasible |
| Core evidence | Crop profile, soil, field/drainage, climate/water, planting material where relevant, and dated market evidence |
| Intended outcome | An explainable feasibility report, evidence-gap list, and human-review record |

## What has already been built

A tested backend foundation is in place. It calculates transparent agronomic, climate, and market components; records evidence quality and review reasons; preserves an audit trace; and uses an AI explanation layer that is not permitted to change the underlying score. The Guyana pilot has been configured so that maize and ginger remain **human-review-required** until locally approved crop profiles are in place.

The next milestone is not broader automation. It is to validate the crop profiles, evidence standards, data pathways, and officer workflow with Guyana stakeholders.

## What the pilot is—and is not

| The pilot is | The pilot is not |
|---|---|
| A pre-plant evidence and feasibility tool | A credit-approval system |
| A support tool for extension officers and agronomists | A replacement for local agronomic judgment |
| A controlled learning exercise for maize and ginger | A national roll-out or generalised crop platform |
| A structured way to identify data gaps | A guarantee of yield, income, weather, market access, or farm success |
| A potential foundation for future responsible services | An insurance quote, buyer match, contract, order, payment, or automated recommendation |

> **Core assurance:** Partners retain control of their expertise and information. AgriScore does not activate a crop profile, conceal uncertainty, or turn a feasibility score into a financial or commercial action without the appropriate review and governance.

## Why we are requesting this meeting

We are seeking guidance and collaboration to ensure that the Guyana pilot is technically credible, practical for field use, and appropriately governed. We would like to understand the right institutional route for validating crop profiles, collecting and interpreting soil evidence, using climate/water information, documenting market observations, and involving extension or training partners.

## Our primary asks

1. **Nominate or refer an appropriate technical focal point** for maize, ginger, soil, extension, crop protection, climate, or market evidence as relevant.
2. **Participate in a short technical scoping workshop** to define the evidence standards and review process for the pilot.
3. **Advise on the minimum safeguards and operating conditions** that must be in place before a small field cohort is engaged.
4. **Identify potential data, training, or operational pathways** that are appropriate for the pilot—not a blanket request for unrestricted data access.
5. **Agree the next accountable action**, owner, and target date at the end of the discussion.

## Desired outcome from this meeting

The meeting will be successful if we leave with a shared understanding of the pilot, confirmation of the appropriate technical contact(s), the partner’s initial view of the proposed role, the questions that require further work, and agreement on the next working session or referral.

## Suggested opening statement

“Thank you for meeting with us. We are building AgriScore as a practical evidence tool to help farmers and officers evaluate a planting plan before substantial resources are committed. We are starting modestly in Guyana with maize and ginger. We have deliberately designed the system so it cannot replace local technical judgment or make financial decisions. We are here because local agricultural expertise must shape the pilot. We would like to understand how we can work with you to validate the right evidence, safeguards, and field workflow before engaging a small pilot cohort.”
