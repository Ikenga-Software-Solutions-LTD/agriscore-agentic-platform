# AgriScore Guyana Pilot — Partner-Specific Discussion Briefs

**Prepared by:** Chinedu Jamike, Libertas Alpha Technologies  
**Use:** Select the relevant section for the stakeholder meeting. Do not send every section to every partner.

## 1. NAREI — technical collaboration brief

### Why NAREI

NAREI is the central technical partner candidate because it is responsible for agricultural research and extension activities, works on crop productivity and diversification, and identifies field crops such as corn and spices among its areas of activity.[1]

### The meeting objective

Seek NAREI’s guidance on how the project should validate Guyana-specific maize and ginger crop profiles, soil and field evidence, ginger plant-health safeguards, and an officer-led review workflow.

### Specific request

| Request | What we need to agree | What we will provide |
|---|---|---|
| Technical focal point | The right contact(s) for maize, ginger, soils, extension, and plant health | Pilot charter, evidence protocol, and a short technical questionnaire |
| Crop-profile review | Owner, reviewer(s), approval process, versioning, and applicability limits | Draft profile templates with no unapproved thresholds activated |
| Soil/field protocol | Minimum soil report fields, sampling/collection expectations, drainage evidence, and escalation rules | Evidence ledger design and pilot checklist |
| Ginger safeguards | Planting-material, disease-risk, hygiene, and field-condition evidence required before review | Ginger assessment pathway and safety controls |
| Field workflow | Suitable officer roles, training needs, candidate pilot regions, and a realistic supervised cohort approach | Visual user journey and frontend MVP brief |

### Questions to ask NAREI

1. Which NAREI functions or officers should own technical review for maize and ginger in this pilot?
2. What evidence must be present before an agronomist can responsibly discuss a pre-plant feasibility assessment?
3. Which soil measurements, units, report formats, and sampling dates are acceptable for this early pilot?
4. What ginger planting-material and plant-health safeguards should be non-negotiable?
5. How should extension officers collect evidence without creating unnecessary work or false precision?
6. What locations or production contexts should be considered—or excluded—from the first pilot?
7. What would make NAREI comfortable that the pilot supports, rather than duplicates, extension and research work?

### Suggested close

“Would NAREI be willing to nominate a focal point and participate in a 60–90 minute technical scoping workshop? The output would be a controlled workplan—not a public commitment or national deployment.”

### Public contact route

NAREI publicly lists `info@narei.org.gy`, Crop Development & Support Services contacts, and regional coordinators. The initial outreach should ask that the request be directed to the appropriate technical function rather than assume a named officer’s availability.[2]

## 2. Hydrometeorological Service — climate and water evidence brief

### Why Hydromet

Hydromet is Guyana’s official provider of weather, water, and climate information and supports agricultural users among its national responsibilities.[3]

### The meeting objective

Understand the correct and appropriate way to incorporate weather, climate, water-risk, warning, and station/grid evidence in a plot-and-planting-window assessment.

### Specific request

| Request | What we need to agree | What we will provide |
|---|---|---|
| Data-pathway discovery | Relevant historical, seasonal, forecast, warning, and station products; geographic/time resolution; availability and use conditions | A limited data inventory and example evidence schema |
| Interpretation boundary | What the system may display as evidence versus what requires professional interpretation | Proposed climate-evidence screens and disclosure wording |
| Location/quality rules | Appropriate treatment of station distance, missing records, timestamps, and source quality | Provenance fields and failure-state design |
| Technical contact | Appropriate follow-up technical counterpart | Brief technical questionnaire; no request for unrestricted access |

### Questions to ask Hydromet

1. Which public or partnership-appropriate products are relevant to a small pre-plant maize/ginger pilot?
2. What are the appropriate spatial and temporal limitations that must be shown to users?
3. What conditions must be met before a location-specific climate statement is used in an assessment?
4. How should warnings, water conditions, or forecast uncertainty be presented responsibly?
5. Is there an existing farmer/extension climate-service initiative that should inform or connect with the pilot?

### Suggested close

“Could we agree a short data-discovery discussion with the appropriate technical team, so that the pilot uses climate evidence in a way that is accurate, transparent, and consistent with Hydromet guidance?”

## 3. Guyana Marketing Corporation / market partners — market evidence brief

### Why GMC and market partners

The feasibility pilot needs dated, locally grounded market evidence. GMC is a relevant market-development partner candidate with a stated focus on resources for non-traditional agricultural enterprises.[4]

### The meeting objective

Define what a trustworthy market observation looks like for the pilot, rather than assume that a single price or general market statement represents commercial viability.

### Specific request

| Request | What we need to agree | What we will provide |
|---|---|---|
| Commodity definition | What maize and ginger form/grade the pilot records: for example, fresh versus processed ginger and intended maize use/form | A controlled commodity-form vocabulary |
| Observation protocol | Source, market/buyer, date, location, currency, unit, collection method, and verification state | Draft market observation template |
| Data cadence | Reasonable collection frequency and refresh expectations for a small pilot | Data-quality and staleness framework |
| Market context | Known constraints such as transport, post-harvest, processing, seasonality, quality, or buyer requirements | A non-commercial pilot scope and market-evidence screen |

### Questions to ask GMC / market partner

1. Which maize and ginger product forms should be distinguished in a first pilot?
2. What sources are credible enough to record a market observation?
3. How frequently must price/demand observations be refreshed to be useful but not burdensome?
4. What market quality, logistics, processing, or post-harvest information materially changes feasibility?
5. Would a named officer or partner be willing to validate a small observation template?

### Suggested close

“We are not asking the pilot to promise a buyer or forecast a price. We are asking for help defining the minimum market evidence that should be present before a farmer and officer treat a crop plan as commercially informed.”

## 4. Ministry of Agriculture leadership — alignment brief

### Why Ministry leadership

The Ministry can advise on the appropriate institutional engagement path, help avoid duplication, and identify whether the proposed collaboration aligns with existing agricultural priorities and safeguards. The Ministry publicly identifies NAREI, Hydromet, GSA, and other agencies within the wider agricultural system.[5]

### The meeting objective

Provide a concise awareness briefing and ask for guidance on the appropriate technical and governance route for a controlled pilot.

### Specific request

1. Confirm whether the proposed engagement sequence is appropriate.
2. Advise whether any formal letter, data-sharing process, approval, or introduction is required before a small supervised pilot.
3. Refer the project to the correct technical agencies/functions where needed.
4. Clarify how the project should communicate its non-financial, decision-support boundary.

### Suggested close

“We are not requesting a broad endorsement today. We are seeking advice on the right path to a well-governed, technically credible, small pilot that is aligned with the Ministry’s agencies and avoids premature claims.”

## 5. Guyana School of Agriculture — capacity and training brief

### Why GSA

GSA contributes to technical capacity, knowledge exchange, and links among farmers, research, education, training, extension, and agricultural professionals.[6]

### The meeting objective

Explore whether GSA can contribute to a later training and simulated workflow workstream once the crop and evidence protocols are technically ready.

### Specific request

1. Advise on practical training material for officers or supervised student/trainee support.
2. Assess whether a simulated evidence-collection exercise could test usability before farmer enrolment.
3. Identify the appropriate faculty or programme contact for a future capacity-building discussion.

## 6. NPPO — ginger plant-health consultation brief

### Why NPPO

NPPO’s functions include plant pest/disease surveillance, commodity inspection, farm/facility certification, and phytosanitary responsibilities.[7]

### The meeting objective

Seek narrow technical guidance on the plant-health and planting-material safeguards that must be considered while drafting the ginger profile. This is particularly important if the pilot may later consider processed/export-oriented or phytosanitary-sensitive pathways.

### Specific request

1. Identify any non-negotiable plant-health evidence or referral needs for a ginger pilot.
2. Advise on boundaries: what the feasibility system may record versus what requires NPPO/qualified plant-health action.
3. Refer the project to the appropriate specialist if a specific disease/pest protocol is needed.
