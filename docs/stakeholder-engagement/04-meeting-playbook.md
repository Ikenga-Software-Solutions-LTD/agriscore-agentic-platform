# AgriScore Guyana Pilot — Stakeholder Meeting Playbook

**Prepared by:** Chinedu Jamike, Libertas Alpha Technologies  
**Meeting format:** 60 minutes; use 75–90 minutes for a technical NAREI scoping workshop  
**Goal:** Leave with a named next action, owner, and target date.

## 1. Before the meeting

| Item | Owner | Complete when |
|---|---|---|
| Confirm attendee names, roles, and decision authority | Libertas Alpha Technologies | Attendee list identifies technical, operational, and approval functions |
| Send a short pre-read | Libertas Alpha Technologies | Core Meeting Brief is shared 2–3 business days ahead; do not overload recipients with the full pack |
| Select partner-specific brief | Meeting lead | NAREI, Hydromet, GMC, Ministry, GSA, or NPPO section is prepared |
| Define one meeting ask | Meeting lead | The desired commitment is written in one sentence before the meeting |
| Prepare a live action register | Note taker | Action, owner, date, and decision fields are ready to complete during the discussion |
| Clarify boundaries | Meeting lead | The team can clearly state that no financial, commercial, or automated action is being requested |

## 2. Standard 60-minute agenda

| Time | Discussion | Facilitator objective | Output |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Welcome and purpose | Establish respect for the partner’s mandate and explain why their guidance is needed | Shared purpose |
| 5–12 min | The problem and the Guyana pilot | Describe maize/ginger pre-plant feasibility in plain language | Common understanding of scope |
| 12–20 min | What has been built and what has not | Show the existing safeguards, human review gate, and pilot limits | Confidence that the request is disciplined |
| 20–35 min | Partner-specific discussion | Ask focused questions about crop profiles, soil, climate, market, training, or plant health | Technical/operating insights |
| 35–47 min | Proposed collaboration and evidence path | Test the specific request and agree what is realistic | Candidate role and workstream |
| 47–55 min | Decisions, risks, and dependencies | Record what is agreed, unresolved, and out of scope | Action register populated |
| 55–60 min | Close and next step | Confirm owner, deliverable, date, and follow-up method | Clear next commitment |

## 3. Opening script (approximately three minutes)

“Thank you for meeting with us. Libertas Alpha Technologies is building AgriScore as a practical support tool for crop planning before planting. We are starting in Guyana with maize and ginger because we want to learn in a focused and responsible way.

The platform brings together evidence about a plot, soil, climate and water conditions, crop requirements, and market observations. It then makes the evidence easier to review: it shows what supports a plan, what information is missing, and what needs professional attention.

We have intentionally designed it with strong limits. It does not approve loans, quote insurance, promise buyers, place orders, or replace extension and agronomic judgment. In Guyana, maize and ginger remain under a human-review gate until local crop profiles are approved.

We are here because a useful pilot must be shaped by Guyana’s institutions and experts. We would like to understand how we can work with you to define the right evidence, review route, and field workflow for a small, supervised pilot.”

## 4. Five points to demonstrate, not merely claim

| Point | How to explain it simply | Supporting material |
|---|---|---|
| Clear pilot boundary | “One country, two crops, pre-plant decision support, small supervised cohort.” | Core Meeting Brief and Pilot Charter |
| Human review control | “If evidence is missing or the crop profile is not locally approved, the system says review is required.” | Guyana pilot configuration and visual user journey |
| Transparent logic | “The system shows the agronomic, climate, and market factors behind the assessment.” | Assessment architecture / evidence protocol |
| Responsible AI | “AI helps explain the result; it cannot change the score or make a financial decision.” | Architecture and safeguards |
| Partner value | “Your guidance becomes a controlled profile and workflow, not an untraceable black box.” | Partner-specific brief and action register |

## 5. Core discovery questions

Use only the questions relevant to the stakeholder. Avoid turning the first meeting into a data-extraction exercise.

| Topic | Questions |
|---|---|
| Mandate and fit | “Where does this proposal align with your current priorities or responsibilities?” “What would be the appropriate internal route for a small technical collaboration?” |
| Technical evidence | “What evidence would you require before a maize or ginger plan can be responsibly discussed?” “What evidence should the platform never infer?” |
| Operational reality | “What would make this workflow practical—or impractical—for officers and farmers?” “Which step is most likely to create burden or inconsistency?” |
| Data and governance | “What information can be used, under what conditions, and with what limitations?” “What must remain controlled by the institution or reviewed by a professional?” |
| Pilot design | “Which locations or production contexts should be considered or excluded initially?” “What size and duration would be responsible for a first supervised pilot?” |
| Next action | “Who should own the next technical step?” “What is the smallest useful next commitment we can agree today?” |

## 6. NAREI technical workshop agenda (90 minutes)

| Time | Activity | Desired result |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Confirm purpose, roles, non-financial boundary, and workshop outputs | Shared rules for the discussion |
| 10–25 min | Review maize pathway: crop profile, soil/field evidence, climate, market, officer workflow | Maize evidence questions and owner |
| 25–45 min | Review ginger pathway: planting material, plant health, drainage/wetness, crop profile, market form | Ginger safeguards and owner |
| 45–60 min | Soil, laboratory, field-observation, and evidence-quality protocol | Minimum report fields, units, dates, methods, quality states |
| 60–72 min | Agronomist review and change-control process | Profile approval and assessment-review path |
| 72–82 min | Candidate locations, officers, training, and small cohort conditions | Controlled pilot operating assumptions |
| 82–90 min | Confirm actions and meeting record | Owners, dates, unresolved risks, next workshop |

## 7. Constructive responses to likely questions

| Stakeholder question or concern | Suggested response |
|---|---|
| “Is this a lending or credit-scoring system?” | “No. The first pilot is limited to pre-plant crop feasibility and evidence quality. It does not approve finance or make any financial recommendation.” |
| “Will the AI replace agronomists or extension officers?” | “No. The system is designed to make their evidence and judgment easier to organise and explain. Qualified reviewers remain central to the process.” |
| “Are you asking for our data?” | “We are first asking for guidance on what is appropriate. Any data use would be specific, minimal, documented, and subject to agreed conditions.” |
| “How do we know the assessment is valid?” | “We do not claim it is valid until local experts review the crop profile and controlled pilot outcomes are compared with professional judgment and field evidence.” |
| “Why maize and ginger?” | “They give the pilot a focused field-crop and spice-crop pathway while keeping profile governance manageable. The selection is a starting scope, not a claim that one profile fits all locations.” |
| “What do you need from us now?” | “A technical focal point, a short scoping session, and advice on the correct standards and pilot pathway. We are not asking for a large commitment today.” |
| “What happens if the system gives a poor result?” | “The result is not a command. It shows the evidence, uncertainty, and questions that require professional or farmer consideration.” |

## 8. Closing script

“Thank you for the guidance today. To make sure we act on it correctly, may we confirm the next step before we close? We would like to record the action, the responsible person or team, the expected output, and a practical target date. We will circulate a concise written summary for confirmation and will not represent any collaboration or endorsement beyond what has been agreed.”

## 9. Meeting conduct rules

The team should listen more than it speaks, avoid claims that cannot yet be validated, distinguish prototypes from operating systems, and accurately record concerns. No participant should be pressed for data, public endorsement, or decisions outside their mandate. If a question cannot be answered, it should be recorded as an action rather than answered speculatively.
